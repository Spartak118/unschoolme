How to Build Credit
From Zero — The Complete Guide
6 min read UnschoolMe Editorial Updated May 2026
Key Takeaways
What you'll know after reading this
✓A secured credit card is the fastest and safest way to start building credit from scratch
✓Becoming an authorized user on someone else's card can instantly add credit history to your file
✓Credit-builder loans are designed specifically for people with no credit history
✓Most people can reach a "Good" credit score (670+) within 12–18 months by following these steps

Why you need credit — even if you hate debt

No credit history is not the same as good credit. To credit bureaus, you're invisible — and invisible people get rejected or pay more for almost everything: apartments, loans, phone plans, even some jobs.

Building credit doesn't mean going into debt. The goal is to create a track record that proves you can borrow responsibly. You can do this spending as little as $10–20 per month.

The catch-22 of credit: You need credit history to get credit — but you can't build history without credit. That's exactly why this guide exists. The three methods below break that cycle.

Step 1 — Get a secured credit card

A secured credit card works like a regular credit card, but you put down a refundable deposit (usually $200–$500) that becomes your credit limit. The card reports to all three credit bureaus — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — and builds your history month by month.

1
Apply for a secured card (no credit check required)
Good options: Discover it® Secured, Capital One Platinum Secured, or OpenSky® Secured Visa. OpenSky doesn't even require a bank account or credit check.
2
Deposit $200–$500 to open the account
This deposit is refundable when you close the account or graduate to a regular card. Think of it as collateral, not a fee.
3
Use it for small, regular purchases
Put one small recurring bill on it — Netflix, groceries, gas. Keep your spending under 30% of your limit (under 10% is ideal).
4
Pay the full balance every month, on time
Set up autopay for the full statement balance. This avoids interest charges and maximizes your payment history score — the most important factor.

Pro tip: After 6–12 months of on-time payments, many secured cards automatically "graduate" you to an unsecured card and refund your deposit. Discover it® Secured reviews your account every 7 months. Capital One reviews after 5 months.

Step 2 — Become an authorized user

If you have a family member or trusted friend with good credit, ask them to add you as an authorized user on their credit card. You don't even need to use the card — their positive payment history immediately appears on your credit report.

Best Option
Parent's card
If they have 5+ years of history and low utilization, this adds instant credibility to your thin file.
Works Well
Spouse/partner
Common strategy for building credit as a couple when one person has better history than the other.
Low Risk
Trusted friend
They can add you without giving you a physical card. Their account history appears on your report at no risk to them.

Important: If the person you piggyback on has bad credit — late payments, high balances — it will hurt your score too. Only become an authorized user on accounts with a clean history.

Step 3 — Open a credit-builder loan

A credit-builder loan is specifically designed for people with no credit. Instead of getting money upfront, you make monthly payments into a locked savings account. When the loan is paid off, you get the money. The lender reports your payments to all three bureaus, building your history.

Self (self.inc) is the most popular option — they offer $25–$150/month plans. Credit unions often offer them too for $500–$1,500 over 12–24 months.

The benefit: you build credit and forced savings at the same time. After 12 months you get your money back (minus a small fee).

The habits that matter

The method you choose matters less than the habits you build around it. These four habits determine 90% of your score trajectory:

  • Never miss a payment. Even one 30-day-late payment can drop your score 100 points and stays on your report 7 years. Set autopay.
  • Keep utilization under 30%. If your limit is $500, never let your balance exceed $150. Under 10% is ideal for a great score.
  • Don't close old accounts. Length of credit history counts. Keep your oldest card open even if you rarely use it.
  • Don't apply for multiple cards at once. Each application is a hard inquiry and slightly dips your score. Space applications 6 months apart.

Your 12-month timeline

Here's a realistic timeline for someone starting from zero:

Month 1
Open your secured card
Apply for Discover it® Secured or Capital One Platinum Secured. Deposit $200–$300. Set up autopay for full balance.
Month 2–3
Your first score appears
After 1–2 billing cycles reporting to bureaus, you'll have a score. Usually 620–650 to start — don't panic, this is normal.
Month 4–6
Add a credit-builder loan
Open a Self credit-builder account ($25/month plan). Now you have two accounts building history simultaneously.
Month 6–8
Score reaches "Fair" range (580–669)
With consistent on-time payments and low utilization, most people cross 600 here. Some landlords and basic cards become accessible.
Month 10–12
Score reaches "Good" range (670+)
You may be offered a card upgrade or credit limit increase. Apply for one unsecured card to add a second line of credit.
Month 18–24
Score reaches "Very Good" (740+)
At this point, you qualify for the best rates on car loans, apartments, and most credit cards. The foundation is built.
