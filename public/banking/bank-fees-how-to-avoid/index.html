Bank Fees That Drain Your Money —
And How to Avoid Every One

Overdraft fees. Monthly fees. ATM fees. Americans pay billions in unnecessary bank fees every year. Here's how to pay zero.

Free 4 min read UnschoolMe Editorial Updated 2025
Key Takeaways
→ US banks collected over $8 billion in overdraft fees in 2023 — that's money from people who could least afford it
→ The average overdraft fee is $35, sometimes for a transaction as small as $1
→ Monthly maintenance fees at big banks run $12–$15/month ($180/year)
→ You can legally opt out of overdraft coverage with a single phone call
→ Switching to Chime, Ally, or a credit union eliminates most fees permanently

Monthly Maintenance Fees — Paying to Have an Account

At many big banks, simply having a checking account costs money. This is the most quietly damaging fee because it's automatic — it comes out whether you use the account or not, and many people don't notice it for months.

Here's what the three largest US banks charge for basic checking in 2024:

BankAccountMonthly FeeHow to Waive It
ChaseTotal Checking$12/month$500+ monthly direct deposit
Bank of AmericaAdvantage Plus$12/month$250+ direct deposit or $1,500 daily balance
Wells FargoEveryday Checking$10/month$500+ direct deposit or $500 daily balance
CitibankRegular Checking$15/month$1,500 combined balance

Even with the waivers available, millions of people pay these fees — either because they fall below the threshold, don't have qualifying direct deposit, or simply don't know the waiver exists. At $12/month, that's $144/year to hold a basic checking account. At $15/month, $180/year. For someone living paycheck to paycheck, this is a meaningful sum taken from the most financially vulnerable customers.

The permanent solution is simple: choose a bank that charges no monthly fee at all. Ally, Chime, Discover, and most credit unions charge $0 with no minimums, no strings, no conditions.

Overdraft Fees — The Biggest Fee Trap in Banking

An overdraft happens when you spend more than your available balance. Banks have two choices: decline the transaction, or let it process and charge you a fee. Most traditional banks choose the fee.

The average overdraft fee is $35 per transaction. Many banks charge this multiple times per day if multiple transactions hit while you're in the negative. A string of small purchases — a $4 coffee, a $12 lunch, a $3 app subscription — can each trigger a separate $35 fee. Your $19 of transactions just cost you $19 + $105 in fees. That's the math.

US banks collected $8.8 billion in overdraft fees in 2022 and approximately $8 billion in 2023 (after regulatory pressure prompted some banks to reduce fees). The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) found that overdraft fees fall disproportionately on households earning under $50,000 — the people least able to absorb a $35 surprise charge.

The Real Cost — Do This Math

One overdraft fee per week = $35 × 52 = $1,820 per year. Many people who struggle with overdrafts aren't irresponsible — they're one unexpected expense away from a negative balance at the wrong moment. The fee structure punishes the unlucky and the under-resourced most severely.

Here's exactly how to protect yourself:

  1. Call your bank today and opt out of overdraft protection. Federal law (Regulation E) requires banks to get your explicit consent before enrolling you in debit card overdraft coverage. If you haven't opted in, they cannot charge you for overdrafts on debit card purchases — the transaction will simply decline. If you've already opted in, call and opt back out. It takes 5 minutes. Declined transactions are embarrassing. $35 fees are worse.
  2. Set up low-balance text alerts. Most banks let you trigger a text when your balance drops below $50 or $100. This gives you time to transfer funds before a transaction hits. Do this today — it's free and takes 2 minutes in your app's settings.
  3. Link a savings account as overdraft backup. Many banks allow you to link a savings account to your checking account. If your checking overdrafts, they pull from savings automatically. This is usually free or costs a flat $5 per transfer — vastly cheaper than $35 per item.
  4. Switch to a bank with no overdraft fees. Chime's SpotMe feature covers up to $200 in overdrafts with no fee if you have qualifying direct deposit. Ally Bank offers a no-fee "overdraft transfer" from savings. These aren't gimmicks — they're permanent policy differences.

ATM Fees — Paying to Access Your Own Money

Every time you use an out-of-network ATM, you're hit with two separate fees: one from your own bank, one from the ATM owner. They stack.

The Real Cost of One Out-of-Network ATM Withdrawal
Your bank's out-of-network fee (avg)$2.50
ATM operator surcharge (avg)$3.08
Total cost per withdrawal$5.58
Weekly withdrawals × 52 weeks$290/year

$290 per year to access your own cash. This is entirely avoidable with four strategies:

  • Use your bank's ATM network. Every major bank has a map in their app showing nearby in-network ATMs. Plan ahead.
  • Get cash back at the grocery store. Walmart, Kroger, Target, CVS, and most grocery stores offer free cash back at checkout when you use your debit card. Ask for $20–$40 back when you pay for groceries. Zero fee, ever.
  • Use the Allpoint or MoneyPass network. These networks have 55,000+ ATMs inside Walgreens, CVS, Target, 7-Eleven, and other retailers nationwide — often free with online bank accounts. Download their apps to find the nearest one.
  • Switch to Ally or Charles Schwab. These banks reimburse all ATM fees nationwide, every month, regardless of which ATM you use. Schwab's checking account is particularly well-regarded for travelers internationally — they reimburse foreign ATM fees too.

Other Fees Most People Don't Know About

Beyond the big three, banks have an extensive menu of smaller fees that quietly drain accounts. Most are completely avoidable once you know they exist.

The foreign transaction fee deserves special attention. If you use a Bank of America or Chase debit card abroad, every purchase — a meal, a cab, a hotel — costs an additional 1–3% on top of whatever you spend. On a $3,000 trip, that's $30–$90 in invisible fees on every swipe. Charles Schwab Bank's checking account charges zero foreign transaction fees and reimburses foreign ATM surcharges worldwide — making it the near-unanimous recommendation for international travelers.

The One Phone Call Worth Making Right Now

Call your bank's customer service number (it's on the back of your debit card) and say: "I want to remove overdraft protection from my account." That's it. They may try to convince you to keep it — decline politely. If your balance ever goes negative, transactions will simply decline instead of processing. A declined transaction is inconvenient. A $35 overdraft fee is expensive. Choose inconvenient.
